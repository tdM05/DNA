For isosceles triangles, the angles at the base are equal to one another, and if the equal sides are produced then the angles under the base will be equal to one another. Let $ABC$ be an isosceles triangle having the side $AB$ equal to the side $AC$, and let the straight-lines $BD$ and $CE$ have been produced in a straight-line with $AB$ and $AC$ (respectively) [Post.~2]. I say that the angle $ABC$ is equal to $ACB$, and (angle) $CBD$  to $BCE$.

new proof here: Since the side $AB$ is equal to the side $AC$, the base angles are equal to one another, so the angle $ABC$ is equal to the angle $ACB$. Now, since the angle $ABC$ together with the angle $CBD$ makes two right-angles, and the angle $ACB$ together with the angle $BCE$ makes two right-angles, and since $ABC$ is equal to $ACB$, the remaining angle $CBD$ is equal to the remaining angle $BCE$. Thus, the angles at the base are equal to one another, and the angles under the base are equal to one another. (Which is) the very thing it was required to show.
