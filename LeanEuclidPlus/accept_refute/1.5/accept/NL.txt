For isosceles triangles, the angles at the base are equal to one another, and if the equal sides are produced then the angles under the base will be equal to one another. Let $ABC$ be an isosceles triangle having the side $AB$ equal to the side $AC$, and let the straight-lines $BD$ and $CE$ have been produced in a straight-line with $AB$ and $AC$ (respectively) [Post.~2]. I say that the angle $ABC$ is equal to $ACB$, and (angle) $CBD$  to $BCE$. 

new proof here: For since $AB$ is equal to $AC$, and $AC$ to $AB$, the two (straight-lines) $BA$, $AC$ are equal to the two (straight-lines) $CA$, $AB$, respectively. And they encompass a common angle, $BAC$. Thus, comparing triangle $ABC$ with triangle $ACB$, the base $BC$ is equal to the base $CB$, and the remaining angles subtended by the equal sides are equal to the corresponding remaining angles [Prop.~1.4]. Thus, the angle $ABC$, subtended by $AC$, is equal to the angle $ACB$, subtended by $AB$. And these are at the base of triangle $ABC$. Again, since $BD$ has been produced in a straight-line with $AB$, the points $A$, $B$, $D$ lie on a straight-line. Thus, the straight-line $CB$, standing on the straight-line $AD$, makes the (sum of the) adjacent angles $ABC$ and $CBD$ equal to two right-angles [Prop.~1.13]. So too, since $CE$ has been produced in a straight-line with $AC$, the points $A$, $C$, $E$ lie on a straight-line, and the straight-line $BC$, standing on the straight-line $AE$, makes the (sum of the) adjacent angles $ACB$ and $BCE$ equal to two right-angles [Prop.~1.13]. Thus, the (sum of the angles) $ABC$ and $CBD$ is equal to the (sum of the angles) $ACB$ and $BCE$. And of these, the angle $ABC$ was shown to be equal to the angle $ACB$. Thus, the remaining angle $CBD$ is equal to the remaining angle $BCE$ [C.N.~3]. And these are under the base. Thus, for isosceles triangles, the angles at the base are equal to one another, and if the equal sides are produced then the angles under the base will be equal to one another. (Which is) the very thing it was required to show.
